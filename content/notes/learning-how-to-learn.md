## Start with the learning goal

There is no single best framework for every goal. This site combines methods for remembering, understanding, and using ideas. The learning sequence is a site design choice; it is not a tested EMBA intervention.

## What the evidence supports

| Goal | Method and evidence | Site implementation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Remember later | Practice testing and distributed practice received high-utility ratings in the Dunlosky review. [Publisher summary](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/journals/pspi/learning-techniques.html). | Answer before revealing feedback; return on later days. |
| Build conceptual recall | Karpicke and Blunt found retrieval advantages over studying with concept maps in science-text experiments. This does not make diagrams useless or establish executive performance benefits. [Paper, pp. 772–775](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2011/2011_Karpicke_Blunt_Science.pdf). | Practice questions include explanations and new cases, not only definitions. |
| Explain relationships | Chi and colleagues studied prompted explanations of a biology text; the small school-student sample limits direct EMBA generalization. [Paper, abstract and discussion](https://education.asu.edu/sites/g/files/litvpz656/files/lcl/chideleeuwchiulavancher_3.pdf). | Explain why an idea works, and where it might fail. |
| Apply at work | Kolb's experience, reflection, conceptualization, and experimentation cycle provides an organizing framework. It is not evidence for this site's reminder intervals. [Authors' guide, introduction and section 1](https://learningfromexperience.com/books/8-things-to-know-about-the-experiential-learning-cycle.pdf). | Record a real situation, interpret it, test a change, and revisit the result. |

The 2022 spacing/retrieval review discusses retention and transfer. Only its public abstract and metadata were accessible here; the full licensed text was not reviewed. [Review](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-022-00089-1).

## The learning loop

```concept-map
title: Read, retrieve, explain, apply — then return
steps:
  - label: Capture and understand
    detail: Read a sourced note and its limitations.
  - label: Retrieve and compare
    detail: Try an answer from memory, then check feedback.
  - label: Explain and experiment
    detail: Connect the idea to a real decision and test a small change.
  - label: Revisit and revise
    detail: Review later, compare outcomes, and adjust your explanation.
caption: A practical synthesis of the selected methods; not an independently validated curriculum.
```

## A short practice session

Open Practice in the navigation. Read a question and answer from memory, in writing or mentally. Reveal the suggested answer, compare it with your attempt, and self-rate honestly. Suggested answers are authored prompts, not an automatic grading system.

Use the workplace reflection panel to separate what happened, what you noticed, your interpretation, a prediction, and the outcome. Leave unknowns blank rather than inventing a result. Compare national and organizational culture questions to practice recognizing the correct level of analysis.

## Review dates and progress

The initial ratings schedule another attempt after 1 day, a partial recall after 3 days, and clear recall after 7 days. Further clear recalls move to 14, 30, and 60 days. These intervals are a transparent product heuristic, not a scientifically optimized prescription or a prediction of your memory. Dates use Toronto time. You can choose All questions at any time.

New questions join the queue automatically. If either language’s prompt or answer changes, its review revision changes and the question becomes due again. English and Chinese versions share progress.

Responses are saved when you rate a question; reflections are saved with Save reflection. Data stays in this browser and does not sync to GitHub or other devices. Export Markdown to keep a copy. Closing the page before saving can lose your draft.

## What this does not prove

A self-rating is not objective mastery, and a completed question is not evidence of business impact. Test transfer using actual decisions and outcomes. This design has not been evaluated against an EMBA control group. Diagrams, tags, and note organization support navigation and explanation; they do not replace retrieval.

## Questions to revisit

- Which errors still occur after a delay?
- Can I explain the concept without its original example?
- Did my workplace experiment change an observable outcome?
- Should the review intervals be shorter for this material?

```learning-practice
objective: Choose learning activities that match recall, understanding, and
  application goals.
application: Pick one concept you need at work. Describe a situation, explain
  the concept, predict a small change, and record what you observe.
questions:
  - id: retrieval-versus-reading
    kind: recall
    prompt: What two methods are the main retention priorities in this site?
    answer: "Retrieval practice and spaced review: try to recall without the note,
      compare feedback, and revisit later. Simply opening or rereading a note is
      not the same as retrieving it."
  - id: explain-and-transfer
    kind: explain
    prompt: Why are recall, explanation, and workplace experiments different
      activities?
    answer: Recall reconstructs knowledge; explanation explores relationships and
      limits; workplace experiments test an interpretation in context. A high
      self-rating does not establish business impact.
  - id: experiment-loop
    kind: transfer
    prompt: How would you turn an EMBA concept into a small workplace experiment?
    answer: Identify a real experience, record observations, form a tentative
      explanation, predict what a small change could do, and compare the
      observed outcome. Do not invent evidence or attribute every change to the
      concept.
```
