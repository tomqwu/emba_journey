## Key takeaway

Hofstede's framework provides a vocabulary for exploring differences between societies. Use it to formulate questions about expectations, then investigate the actual team or business context. A country-level pattern does not establish how a particular person thinks or behaves.

The supplied website introduces Geert Hofstede (1928–2020), a Dutch social psychologist, and Gert Jan Hofstede's work. This note focuses on its national-culture framework. [Starting source](https://geerthofstede.com/).

## What culture means here

Culture refers to learned, shared expectations about social life. The site distinguishes this shared background from an individual's personality. Its focus includes everyday interactions, beyond culture in the artistic sense. [Source: What is culture](https://geerthofstede.com/culture-geert-hofstede-gert-jan-hofstede/definition-culture/).

## The six dimensions

These are brief paraphrases of the site's explanations, retaining its dimension names.

| Dimension | What it explores |
| --- | --- |
| Individualism / collectivism | Independence versus membership in interdependent social groups. |
| Power distance | Acceptance and expectation of unequal power by those with less power. |
| Masculinity / femininity | Social expectations around gender roles, toughness, winning, and sympathy for others; not an individual's sex or personality. |
| Uncertainty avoidance | Responses to ambiguity and the unknown; distinct from risk avoidance. |
| Long-term / short-term orientation | Adapting and preparing for change versus treating inherited traditions as a moral reference. |
| Indulgence / restraint | Freedom to enjoy life versus emphasis on duty and restriction. |

The website uses approximate 0–100 scales for comparison. It describes national maps as rough patterns with substantial variation within countries, and acknowledges that other dimensions can be constructed. [Source: 6D model, “Dimension maps” and “The dimensions explained”](https://geerthofstede.com/culture-geert-hofstede-gert-jan-hofstede/6d-model-of-national-culture/).

## Levels matter

The site's multilevel discussion distinguishes individuals, groups, and attributes shared by humans. **Learning implication:** do not substitute national averages for observation of individuals or an organization's working practices. [Source: Levels of analysis](https://geerthofstede.com/research-and-vsm/levels-of-analysis/).

## Application: a cross-border project team

*Hypothetical example and agent interpretation; not an experience supplied by the learner.*

A project manager notices that some colleagues challenge proposals openly while others raise concerns privately. Rather than assigning behavior to nationality, ask:

- Who is expected to make the decision, and how can people safely disagree?
- Which decisions need more explanation or predictability?
- What do people understand by individual ownership and collective responsibility?

Test practical changes: collect written concerns before meetings, clarify decision ownership, and agree how uncertainty will be communicated. Compare observed participation and delivery afterward. Treat culture as one possible explanation alongside role, incentives, language, and team history.

```learning-practice
objective: Distinguish national patterns from individual behavior and use
  cultural dimensions to ask better team questions.
application: Choose a real cross-border interaction. What did you observe, what
  else could explain it, and what small change could you test?
questions:
  - id: level-of-analysis
    kind: recall
    prompt: Why can a national cultural score not establish how one colleague will
      behave?
    answer: It describes a group-level pattern, with variation within countries.
      Individual behavior also depends on personality, roles, incentives,
      language, and context.
  - id: uncertainty-and-risk
    kind: explain
    prompt: Explain why uncertainty avoidance and risk avoidance are different. Give
      a possible business example.
    answer: Uncertainty avoidance concerns ambiguity and the unknown, whereas risk
      involves possible outcomes. A team could prefer a clearly specified
      experiment that still has a chance of failure. The example is an
      interpretation, not a country prediction.
  - id: meeting-transfer
    kind: transfer
    prompt: A colleague avoids disagreeing in a meeting. What would you ask or
      change before attributing this to nationality?
    answer: Ask how decisions and disagreement are handled. Investigate role,
      incentives, language, and psychological safety. Try collecting concerns
      before meetings and observe whether participation changes.
```
