## Key takeaway

The site's organizational framework examines shared practices rather than the national-culture dimensions. The research found common understandings of practices even without shared values. [Source: “The organizational culture project”](https://geerthofstede.com/culture-geert-hofstede-gert-jan-hofstede/6-dimensions-organizational-culture/).

## Six practice dimensions

The source identifies these pairs:

- Process-oriented versus results-oriented.
- Job-oriented versus employee-oriented.
- Professional versus parochial.
- Open versus closed systems.
- Tight versus loose control.
- Pragmatic versus normative.

These are research descriptors, not a universal ranking of good and bad cultures.

## Evidence and limitations

The project studied 20 organizational units in Denmark and the Netherlands during the 1980s. The source cautions that this sample was not representative of all organizations and that applicability in different countries cannot be assumed. Industry and other organizational characteristics also matter. [Source: project scope and limitations](https://geerthofstede.com/culture-geert-hofstede-gert-jan-hofstede/6-dimensions-organizational-culture/).

## Application: compare stated values with daily work

*Hypothetical exercise and agent interpretation.*

A company says it values openness. Examine how a new employee actually gets information, asks questions, and raises a concern. Use concrete examples from onboarding and meetings before assigning a cultural label. Select one practice to improve and check whether the employee experience changes.

This exercise is a discussion prompt, not a validated culture survey or a scoring method.
